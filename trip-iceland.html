Fire, Ice & Northern Lights

7 days · 6–12 February 2027 · from £2,320

The idea

Iceland in February is dark for a lot of the day, which sounds like a drawback and isn't. You get low gold light for hours, ice caves that only exist in winter, and a genuine chance at the aurora on any clear night.

This is the easiest trip I run — short drives, no hiking to speak of, good heating. The only thing it asks of you is a willingness to be outside at eleven at night when somebody shouts.

One honest caveat: nobody can promise you the northern lights. We build in five nights and drive to clear sky when there is some, which is about as much as anyone can do.

Day by day

  1. 1

    Arrive Reykjavík

    Transfer in from Keflavík, an easy afternoon in town, and dinner. First aurora watch if the forecast is kind.

  2. 2

    The Golden Circle

    Þingvellir, Geysir and Gullfoss, all of them better in winter. Night in the countryside away from the city lights.

  3. 3

    South coast waterfalls

    Seljalandsfoss and Skógafoss, then the black sand at Reynisfjara. Hot spring in the evening.

  4. 4

    Glacier lagoon and the ice cave

    Jökulsárlón and the Diamond Beach in the morning, into a glacier cave in the afternoon with a certified guide.

  5. 5

    Vík and the sea cliffs

    A slow day working back west, with a walk out to Dyrhólaey and time in Vík.

  6. 6

    Snæfellsnes peninsula

    Kirkjufell, the lava fields, and the black church at Búðir. Last night out under the sky.

  7. 7

    Home

    Back to Keflavík, with time for the lagoon on the way if your flight is late enough.
